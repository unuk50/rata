# Event types

Each group starts with one question: "What type of event is this?" The answer sets the rules, the defaults and the main screen. A poker night and a family vacation need different screens.

| Type | Settings at creation | What changes in the app | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home** (flat, couple, family house) | Default split (equal, shares or income ratio). Repeating bills. Monthly budget. | Repeating bills show first. "Add expense" uses the default split. | Cost per person per month. Bills due next month. |
| **Vacation** | Dates. Trip currency. Split rule: equal for all, by what each person had, or by family unit. Optional shared kitty. Budget. | The split rule sets the add screen. "Equal for all" hides the split step. "By family unit" shows families, not people. | Cost per person per day. Budget used. One settle-up at the end. |
| **Poker** | Minimum and maximum buy-in. Rebuys on or off. Chip value. Food pot. | A buy-in pad replaces "Add expense". At the end, each player enters a cash-out count. | Net result for each player. Fewest transfers. Season table. |
| **Party** | Host. Guests. Who pays the venue. Contributions on or off. | Guests add "I brought" items. The host sees who paid. | Cost per guest. Open contributions. |
| **Gift pool** | Recipient (hidden from the recipient). Target amount. | A progress bar to the target. The recipient never sees the group. | Who paid. Amount left. |
| **Custom** | Start from any type and change its settings. | Follows the settings. | Follows the settings. |

## Rules for poker

- A buy-in below the minimum is not possible. A buy-in above the maximum needs the host to approve.
- The game cannot close until chips out equal chips in. The app shows the difference and the player with the open count.
- Net results are gains and losses, not spend.
- Food and drinks go in a separate pot.

## Rules for family units

- A family unit is a set of people with one person who pays and receives for the unit.
- A child can be part of a unit without an account.
- You can use the same family unit in more than one group.
